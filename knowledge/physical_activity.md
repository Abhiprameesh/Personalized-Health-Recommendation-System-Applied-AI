# Physical Activity and Exercise Guidelines

## Overview
Regular physical activity is one of the most important preventive actions individuals can take for their health. Adults should aim for a combination of aerobic and muscle-strengthening activities tailored to their baseline fitness and health goals.

## Core Adult Recommendations
1. **Aerobic Activity**:
   - Adults should accumulate at least 150 to 300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week, or 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous-intensity aerobic physical activity, or an equivalent combination.
   - Moderate activities include brisk walking (3.0 to 4.5 mph), recreational cycling, water aerobics, or active yard work.
   - Vigorous activities include running, lap swimming, fast cycling, or high-intensity interval training.

2. **Muscle Strengthening**:
   - Adults should engage in muscle-strengthening activities of moderate or greater intensity involving all major muscle groups (legs, hips, back, abdomen, chest, shoulders, arms) on 2 or more days per week.

3. **Starting Safely and Progressive Overload**:
   - For individuals transitioning from low activity, start low and go slow. Initial sessions can be as brief as 10 to 15 minutes of brisk walking.
   - Increase duration and frequency gradually before increasing intensity. For example, progress from 30 minutes of brisk walking 3 to 5 days a week to structured interval walks over a 2 to 4-week adaptation window.
   - For moderate-activity individuals focusing on endurance (e.g., cycling or jogging), maintain 40 to 60-minute sessions 3 to 4 times per week, balanced with dedicated rest and recovery days.

4. **Sedentary Behavior Reduction**:
   - Replacing sedentary time with physical activity of any intensity (including light movement) provides health benefits and reduces cardiovascular risk.
   - Incorporate short 2-minute movement or posture breaks after every 45 to 60 minutes of seated work.
