# General Dietary and Nutrition Guidance

## Overview
A healthy, balanced dietary pattern provides essential energy, macronutrients, and micronutrients needed for optimal physiological function, chronic disease prevention, and sustained energy levels.

## Core Nutritional Principles
1. **Dietary Pattern Diversity**:
   - Emphasize whole, nutrient-dense foods including a colorful variety of vegetables, whole fruits, legumes, whole grains (oats, brown rice, quinoa), nuts, and seeds.
   - Limit added sugars to less than 10% of total daily caloric intake.
   - Keep saturated fat intake below 10% of total calories, substituting with unsaturated fats (olive oil, avocados, seeds).
   - Moderate sodium intake to below 2,000 to 2,300 mg daily.

2. **Dietary Preferences & Plant-Based Patterns**:
   - **Vegetarian and Plant-Forward Diets**: Ensure adequate intake of plant-derived proteins, dietary iron, zinc, and vitamin B12. Excellent plant-based protein sources include lentils, chickpeas, beans, tofu, edamame, tempeh, Greek yogurt, and hemp seeds. Pair non-heme iron sources (spinach, legumes) with vitamin C rich foods (citrus, bell peppers, tomatoes) to enhance absorption.
   - Balanced macronutrient distribution should account for personal goals: roughly 45–55% carbohydrates from complex sources, 20–30% quality proteins, and 25–30% healthy fats.

3. **Hydration**:
   - Maintain baseline hydration of approximately 2.0 to 2.5 liters of water daily for adults, adjusting upwards for heat, humidity, and strenuous exercise.

4. **Allergy and Intolerance Safeguards**:
   - Dietary suggestions must strictly avoid declared food allergens (e.g., peanuts, tree nuts, dairy, soy, wheat/gluten, shellfish, eggs, sesame).
   - Substitute allergenic proteins with safe alternatives (e.g., seed butters for nut allergies, pea or hemp protein for dairy/soy intolerances).
